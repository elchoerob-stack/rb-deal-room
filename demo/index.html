The problem

A sale lives in six places at once

The offer is in somebody's email. The FICA documents are on WhatsApp. The rates clearance is with the attorney, who will tell you when you phone. The compliance certificates are on a desk. The seller rings the agent to ask what is happening, and the agent rings the attorney to find out.

Nobody is doing anything wrong. There is simply no one place where a sale lives, so every answer costs a phone call — and the things that go badly wrong are the ones nobody was watching: a bond condition that lapsed on Friday, a sole mandate that ran out while the house sat on the books, a file that stopped moving three weeks ago and nobody noticed because nobody was looking at all of them at once.

Built here, for here

It follows the South African sequence, not a foreign one

Most property software is written for somewhere else and bent to fit. This one was built around the transfer as it actually runs in this country.

The seven stages

OTP & FICA, bond & deposit, compliance certificates, rates & SARS clearances, drafting & signature, Deeds Office lodgement, registration & handover. Each with the tasks that belong to it and who owns each one.

The certificates

Electrical, gas, plumbing, borer beetle and electric fence — the seller's cost, all five, and nothing lodges without them. The app knows which ones a given property needs.

POPIA

Role-based access to documents, links that expire after fifteen minutes, and an append-only audit trail. A principal sees every file's progress but not the parties' FICA and finance documents — people reach only the personal information they actually need.

The ECT Act

A mandate may be signed on screen. An Offer to Purchase may not — s 4(4) and Schedule 2 exclude the alienation of land from electronic signature — so the app offers signing on the one and prints hand-signing instructions on the other.

Commission as it is really done

A percentage or a fixed fee, VAT inclusive or on top, split between the agency, the agent, a partner agency and a referring agent. Visible to the principal, the deal's agent and anyone named in a split — never to a client, on any screen or document.

Your board, brought across

A Trello export comes over whole: lists, cards, labels, due dates, members, checklists. Pasted-in call notes are read back into structure — the owner gets a Call and a WhatsApp button per number, and the notes stay notes.

Being straight with you

What it does not do

Worth reading before the price, not after.

  • It does not talk to the Deeds Office. There is no live feed of lodgement status in South Africa that a small agency can plug into. The attorney ticks the stage; the app records who ticked it and when.
  • It does not send WhatsApp messages by itself. It writes the message and hands it to WhatsApp with the text filled in. You press send. Automated sending needs a paid WhatsApp Business account, which is a separate decision.
  • It does not replace your deeds-office attorney, your bond originator, or Property24. It is the place where everything about a sale is kept and watched, not a new channel to market on.
  • It cannot see what nobody wrote down. A lead taken on a phone call and never put on a board does not appear in any figure on the agency screen. The numbers are honest about this — where the app has not been told something, it leaves the space blank and says so rather than showing a nought.
  • It is not finished. It is a working system, used on real files, that will keep changing. The plan says how that works and the proposal says who pays for it.
