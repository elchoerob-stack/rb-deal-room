Rollout plan · RB Brand Real Estate

From a demonstration to the thing the agency runs on

About four weeks, in six phases. The principle running through all of them: one agent, one real file, all the way through, before anybody else is asked to change how they work. An agency that moves everybody at once discovers the problems with eight people watching.

Nothing in phase 0 or 1 costs the agency a working hour. The first time an agent has to do anything differently is phase 3, and by then the system already has your houses in it.

The six phases

What happens, and who does it

  1. This week · Ruan

    Decide

    Open the demonstration and push it about — particularly the agency screen and what a purchaser sees, because those are the two that decide it. Tell me what is missing and what is wrong. Pick one of the two ways of paying in the proposal, or tell me neither works.

    Ends when: you say yes, no, or "yes but".

  2. Week 1 · Me, about half a day

    Set it up

    A Google Firebase project in the agency's name, the security rules published, the web address pointed at it, and the first administrator created. Then the roster: every agent and the principal entered, at the level they should see.

    You do: tell me who is on the team and what each of them should be able to see — agent, principal or administrator. One list, five minutes.

    Ends when: you can sign in with your own Google account and see an empty agency.

  3. Week 1–2 · Me, with Charné for an hour

    Bring the work across

    Charné's Trello board is exported and imported whole — lists, cards, labels, due dates, members, checklists, and the pasted-in call notes read back into owners and numbers. Every house currently on the books goes on, with its mandate date. Every sale currently in transfer gets a deal room opened at whatever stage it is honestly at.

    You do: nothing. Charné spends an hour telling me what stage each live file is really at.

    Ends when: the agency screen shows your real pipeline instead of a sample one.

  4. Week 2–3 · Charné, on one real sale

    One agent, one file, all the way

    The next offer Charné signs is run entirely through the app: the purchaser, the seller and the transferring attorney are each invited into that one room and nowhere else. She works it the way she would work it anyway, and every time the app makes something harder instead of easier, she tells me and I fix it that week.

    This is the phase that decides everything. If a real attorney will not use it, or a real seller finds it confusing, better to find out on one file than on eleven.

    Ends when: that file reaches registration, or gets far enough that everybody involved is comfortable.

  5. Week 3–4 · The team

    Everybody on

    Each agent signs in once with their Google account and waits; you approve them from the Staff screen and they are in. One session of about forty-five minutes for the team, and after that the How this works screen inside the app explains every screen in plain language, per role, and the Getting started checklist ticks itself off as each person does the thing.

    You do: approve people, and decide whether an agent may see the whole agency or only their own files. Most agencies choose only their own.

    Ends when: every agent has opened a deal room of their own.

  6. Week 4 onward · Everybody

    Clients in

    Purchasers and sellers get a personal link to their own file and nothing else. This is where the agency stops fielding "what is happening with my transfer" calls, because the answer is on a page the client can open at eleven at night.

    Start with the easy ones. A purchaser who is already comfortable on a phone, on a file that is going smoothly. Difficult clients later, once the pattern is known.

Running it

After the four weeks

A monthly half hour

What broke, what is annoying, what the agency now wishes it did. Small changes go in that month. Big ones get quoted.

Backups

Firestore keeps the data; I take an export monthly and keep it somewhere the agency can reach without me. The app also exports any file or the whole workspace to a file you keep.

Handing over

Any administrator can make another person an administrator and then remove themselves. The agency is never locked to one person, including me.

Being straight with you

What could go wrong

Four real risks, and what is actually done about each.

The riskHow likelyWhat is done about it
The attorney will not use it
The transferring attorney has their own system and no reason to learn yours.		 Likely The attorney is never required to. An agent can tick the attorney's stages on their behalf from a phone call, and the file still holds the documents and the dates. The app is better when the attorney joins; it works when they do not.
The team drifts back to WhatsApp
The old way is in everyone's thumbs.		 Likely Phase 4 is not a training session, it is a habit: Today is the first screen of the morning, because it holds things nobody else is tracking — bond dates, lapsing mandates, stalled files. The app does not try to replace the team's chat; it replaces the hunting.
One person becomes the single point of failure
Me.		 Possible The whole app is one file of plain HTML in a repository the agency owns, with no build step and no dependencies. Any competent developer can open it and work on it. The data lives in the agency's own Google project, not mine. Nothing here needs me to keep running.
A POPIA complaint
A client's personal information in a system the agency did not previously have.		 Unlikely, serious Role-based access, expiring document links and an append-only audit log are built in, and a principal cannot open a client's FICA documents. What the agency still owes: telling clients what is held and why, and naming an information officer. That is the agency's duty under the Act, not the software's, and it exists already whether or not this app does.

What I need from you to start

  • A yes, and which of the two ways of paying.
  • The list of who is on the team and what each one should see.
  • A Google account for each of them — the ones the office already uses are fine.
  • An hour of Charné's time to bring the board and the live files across.
  • One real sale, in week two, run all the way through.
