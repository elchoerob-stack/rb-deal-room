Quoted separately

Syndication: listings out, and leads back in

You asked what it would take to feed listings to the portals directly — to stop paying PropCon's R1 870 and to own the leads that come back. Here is the honest answer, with the part that does not work first.

It does not pay for itself on the PropCon saving. Dropping PropCon saves R1 870 a month. A feed needs a server running on a schedule, which this app currently does not — call it R450 a month in Google Cloud for the feed and the image hosting. So the real saving is about R1 420 a month, or R17 040 a year. Against a R65 000 build that is nearly four years to break even, and against all three stages it is over seven. If saving the R1 870 is the reason, do not build this. Stay on PropCon for syndication and let the Deal Room do the transfer.

The reason to build it is the leads

How it works today

A buyer enquires on Property24. The enquiry goes to the portal, then to whoever runs your feed, then to an agent's inbox. By the time it reaches the agent it is an email. Nobody can say, at the end of a quarter, which portal produced a registered sale — only which produced clicks.

Whatever RB Brand spends on Property24 and Private Property each month — and it is a great deal more than R1 870 — that question is currently unanswerable.

How it would work

The enquiry lands in the Deal Room instead: stamped with the portal it came from, attached to the house it was about, assigned to an agent, and on their Today screen within the minute. It then travels the funnel that already exists on the agency screen — lead, mandate, offer, transfer, registered.

Six months in, Where the business comes from stops being a guess and starts being a number: this portal produced eleven leads, two mandates and one registration worth R41 300 to the agency. That is what you are buying.

What actually has to be built

Four pieces, and the first one is the one that catches people out.

  1. Approval · weeks, not code

    Getting admitted as a feed provider

    Private Property publishes the route: you email feeds@privateproperty.co.za and they send back the API document and the onboarding steps. It is offered to service providers to the estate agent industry, so the agency's own system has to be accepted as one.

    Property24 does not publish an API. There is no developer portal to sign up to — integration is an arrangement with them or with one of their approved providers, and the terms have to be confirmed before a line is written. This is the risk in the whole quote, and it is not mine to control. If they say no, stage 2 does not happen and you are not charged for it.

  2. Build

    A feed service, which means a server

    Today the app is one file with no backend — part of why it is cheap to run and impossible to break. A feed changes that: something has to wake on a schedule, build an XML document per portal in that portal's own schema, and serve or push it. That is a small scheduled service in Google Cloud, and it is the R450 a month.

  3. Build

    Images on the internet, and a mapping layer

    Portals fetch photographs by URL, so the houses need real image hosting — the app has none today. Then every field has to be translated into each portal's own vocabulary: their property types, their listing statuses, and their suburb codes, which are not your suburb names. Create, update, price change, withdrawn and sold all have to travel correctly, or a sold house stays advertised.

  4. Build

    Validation you can see

    A feed that silently drops four listings is worse than no feed, because nobody finds out until an owner asks why their house is not on Property24. So: a screen that shows what went out, what was rejected and why, and a message to the agent whose listing it was.
