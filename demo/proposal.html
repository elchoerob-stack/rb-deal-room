The system in the demonstration already exists and already works. It was built around how this agency actually sells houses in Pretoria East, not adapted from something written for somewhere else. What this proposal buys is the work of putting it under your agency's own control, moving the work of your eleven agents into it, getting them onto it, and keeping it running and changing as you need it to.

The price is further up than the listing systems you already know, so the next section puts their published prices beside it and says plainly what each of us does and does not do. Judge the number after that, not before.

Scope

What you get

The system itself

  • Deal rooms — seven stages from OTP to registration, with tasks owned per party
  • Document vault with role-based access and links that expire
  • Houses on the books, with mandate clocks, viewings, offers and show-day registers
  • The prospecting board, with your Trello import
  • Today, the diary, and the mandate watch
  • Commission and the money screen, per agent
  • The agency screen — your numbers
  • Paperwork filled on screen, printed or signed where the law allows it
  • Works on a phone, installs to the home screen, works without signal

The work of putting it in

  • A Google Firebase project in the agency's name, with the security rules published
  • Your own web address pointed at it
  • Your Trello board imported whole
  • Every house on the books and every live transfer loaded at its real stage
  • The team set up at the right access levels
  • One session of about forty-five minutes with the team
  • One real sale walked through end to end with Charné, with fixes that week
  • A monthly export of everything, kept where the agency can reach it without me

What none of them do

  • The transfer itself. Seven stages from OTP to registration, with the purchaser, the seller and the attorney each in the room, each seeing their own part.
  • A client who can look for themselves. Their steps, their documents, their thread, on a page they can open at eleven at night — and nothing about the commission or the other party.
  • A POPIA audit trail that records every upload, every view and every message, with who and when.
  • Your own numbers. The funnel from a name on a board to a registered sale, per agent, with where the drop is.
  • Changes you ask for. PropCon will not alter their system for an eleven-agent agency in Pretoria East. I will, and that is most of what the monthly is.

What they do that this does not

  • Syndicate your listings to Property24 and Private Property. PropCon does it free. This does not do it at all.
  • Run your public website. Also not this.
  • Bulk SMS and mail campaigns out of the box.
So read this honestly. If the agency is on PropCon or something like it today, the Deal Room goes alongside it, not instead of it — R7 500 on top of R1 870, not replacing it. It earns that by being the part none of them cover: the sale after the offer is signed. If you would rather it replaced the lot, syndication is the piece to build, and I will quote it.

What a year of it is worth

At an average registered sale of R1,9m and a five per cent fee split evenly, the agency's own half is about R41 300. A year of Option A is R90 000 — a little over two of those.

So it pays for itself if, across eleven agents in a year, it saves two sales that would otherwise have gone: a bond condition nobody was watching, a sole mandate that lapsed while the house sat on the books, a file that stopped moving and was only noticed at the month-end meeting. Whether it does that is the only question worth arguing about.

What it costs me to run

An agency this size sits inside Google Firebase's free tier — about R0 a month until roughly fifty thousand document reads a day, which eleven agents will not reach — and a domain is around R250 a year.

So the monthly is not paying for servers. It is paying for me: on the end of a phone, fixing what breaks, and building what the agency asks for next. You should know that when you judge whether the number is fair. If you outgrow the free tier I will tell you before it happens, not after.

Not included

What is outside this price

  • Automated WhatsApp sending. Needs a paid WhatsApp Business account and approved message templates. About R2 000 to set up and a few cents a message. A separate decision, once the agency knows it wants it.
  • A feed from Property24 or Private Property. Their export agreements are between them and the agency. If you get one, wiring it in is quoted then.
  • Accounting integration. The money screen tells an agent what is theirs; it is not a bookkeeping system and does not pretend to be.
  • Anything with a new party in it — a bank, a bond originator, a municipality — is new work and is quoted when you want it.

Terms

The things that matter if it goes wrong

The data is the agency's

It lives in a Google Firebase project registered to RB Brand Real Estate, not to me. If we part company you change the password and I am out. I keep no copy.

You can walk away with it

Any administrator can export the whole workspace to a file at any time, from inside the app, without asking me. Under Option B the source code is yours as well.

POPIA

The software gives you role-based access, expiring links and an audit trail. Naming an information officer and telling clients what you hold remains the agency's duty under the Act — it is yours already, with or without this.

Support

Phone and WhatsApp, answered the same working day. Something that stops the agency working gets fixed that day. Everything else goes on the monthly list.

If it does not work out

Under Option A, thirty days' notice and nothing further is owed. Under Option B, if the agency is not running on it by the end of week six, the once-off is refunded in full — I would rather give the money back than have it sitting unused.

Payment

Setup or once-off on acceptance. Monthly by debit order from the first full month after phase 4. All figures exclude VAT.
